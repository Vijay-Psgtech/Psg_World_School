Standardised Growth Tracking

NWEA MAP Growth

Most schools test what students have been taught. MAP Growth measures something different — it measures where a child is right now, and how far they have travelled since the last assessment.

Developed by NWEA and used by over 13 million students worldwide, MAP Growth adapts in real time as a child answers. If a question is too easy, the next is harder. If it is too hard, the next adjusts accordingly. The result is a precise picture of each child's actual level — not a score relative to a curriculum, but a position on a continuous scale that spans from Early Years to Grade 12.

At PSG World School, students sit MAP Growth assessments three times a year. Teachers use the data not to rank children, but to understand exactly what each child needs next — and to identify growth that a single end-of-year exam would never reveal.

⊞
Adaptive by design
Every question adjusts to the child's responses. The assessment meets each student at their level — not the class average.
↑
Growth, not just attainment
MAP tracks progress over time. A child who starts behind can show exceptional growth — and that growth is visible, measurable, and celebrated.
◉
Three times a year
Autumn, spring, and summer. Each sitting produces a fresh data point — so teachers always have a current picture, not a year-old one.
✎
Informs teaching directly
Results are not filed away. Teachers use MAP data to differentiate instruction — adjusting what they teach, to whom, and at what pace.

Progress is never a mystery at PSG World School. You will always know how your child is growing — not through end-of-year reports, but in real time, every week, through their Toddle portfolio.

"In 1926, a member of the Naidu family was refused admission to a British-run school in Coimbatore. Four brothers divided their ancestral property into five shares. That refusal, carried forward across a century, became one of India's most significant charitable educational trusts: 33 institutions, 26,000 patients served at Vedapatti alone, the school you are reading about now. The founding question has not changed.
